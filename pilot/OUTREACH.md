# Origin discovery campaign

## Objective

Secure one qualified origin design partner for a bounded, non-enforcing shadow evaluation. The campaign is successful when an origin owner commits to a concrete integration step—not when people express general interest.

## Target segments

Prioritize origins with bot pressure and legitimate delegated demand:

1. Travel or hospitality availability.
2. Appointment or reservation discovery.
3. Marketplace or retail inventory lookup.
4. Ticket or event availability, excluding purchase in the first pilot.
5. Developer platforms where agent traffic encounters web-only boundaries.

Avoid beginning with payments, account recovery, healthcare writes, legal acceptance, or physical control.

## Target roles

- Head or lead of bot management, trust and safety, fraud, identity, or platform security.
- Product owner for conversion affected by challenges.
- Developer-platform or edge-infrastructure lead.
- Support leader with evidence of legitimate automation blocks.

## Initial message

> We are developing AgentIsOK, an open request-bound alternative to CAPTCHA for agents acting under bounded authority. The origin remains the final decision-maker, and the verifier can run without an AgentIsOK service. We are looking for one low-consequence workflow where legitimate agent traffic is currently blocked or challenged. The first evaluation is shadow-only: your existing controls remain authoritative. Would you be willing to discuss the last concrete incident and what evidence would have changed the decision?

Do not lead with protocol features or standardization. Lead with the origin's last concrete failure and the safety of shadow evaluation.

## Campaign stages

### Stage 1: 20 prospects

Build a small, attributable list across the target segments. Record why each prospect is likely to see the problem and which role owns it. Do not scrape personal contact data into the repository.

### Stage 2: 10–15 interviews

Use [ORIGIN-INTERVIEW.md](ORIGIN-INTERVIEW.md). Separate workflow evidence from enthusiasm. Ask permission before naming an organization publicly.

### Stage 3: three technical follow-ups

For the strongest candidates, produce a one-page action map:

- protected action;
- incumbent control point;
- challenge and evidence requirements;
- prohibited data;
- shadow placement;
- outcome labels;
- rollback owner; and
- stop/continue criteria.

### Stage 4: one pilot

Select the partner with the strongest combination of materiality, origin ownership, safe integration, and outcome evidence—not necessarily the most recognizable name.

## Public call for participation

Open one repository issue titled **Seeking an origin design partner for a shadow-mode pilot**. Link to the pilot packet and ask respondents to use the origin-workflow template. Keep confidential operational details out of the issue.

Share the call selectively with origin operators, agent harness projects, HTTP Message Signatures implementers, bot-management practitioners, and OAuth/WIMSE participants. Do not describe exploratory interoperability as standards endorsement.

## Stop condition

After 10–15 qualified interviews, stop or substantially reshape if origins cannot name desirable traffic, will not expose a safe shadow point, or consistently prefer first-party APIs, ordinary OAuth, Web Bot Auth alone, or their incumbent provider.
